## 1. The decision

Position the business as **websites and ongoing digital support for Australian small businesses**. Make new websites and redesigns the main acquisition offers. Present hosting and maintenance as continuity after launch; SEO as a separate programme; and AI marketing as specific, scoped services rather than a vague promise of automated growth.

Lead with small service businesses. They have a coherent need: explain their services, show credible work, receive enquiries and keep the website maintained. Trades can be the first industry experiment, but they should not define the entire business or be described as an easy SEO niche.

Use Sydney as a customer market, and Australia as the service coverage. Do not claim a Sydney office or Australian physical presence. The operating context is remote delivery from Nepal. Use “for Sydney businesses” and “serving Australian businesses”. Keep the existing domain: treating search traction as new does not mean discarding the domain, existing links or useful URLs.

The business started in 2017. That is legitimate history; it is not evidence of SEO authority. The owner's report of no traffic is the planning baseline, not a verified analytics finding.

### Commercial positioning

- Primary offer: a clear, professional small-business website or redesign, with an agreed scope.
- Differentiation to implement: direct communication, explicit inclusions, useful service copy, straightforward enquiry paths and optional ongoing support.
- Avoid leading on “cheapest”. No new price promise has been authorised or costed.
- Old prices are not carried into this fresh strategy. Use project-specific quotes until scope, delivery costs and ongoing charges are established.
- Do not promote app development, enterprise automation, paid-ad management or ecommerce as standalone services merely because competitors do. They are outside the confirmed core brief.

### How keywords were selected

1. Start with the confirmed services, not industry buzzwords.
2. Add customer needs: new website, outdated website, ongoing help, better visibility, enquiry handling.
3. Inspect provider pages and results to distinguish service-buying intent from software shopping, learning or jobs.
4. Group close variants around one useful page. Do not create one page per phrase.
5. Prioritise clear fit and credible deliverability while demand/competition metrics remain pending.
6. Validate numerical demand and organic competition before calling any term a low-competition opportunity.

### Publishing order

Core launch: Home, Website Packages, Website Redesign, Hosting & Care, SEO, AI Marketing, About and Contact — eight pages. Add the tradie page and two guides when their substance is ready. A portfolio is an evidence gate: prepare genuine case studies before adding the page to navigation. Privacy/terms must reflect the actual tools, data handling and commercial arrangements; this document does not invent them.

Navigation: **Websites** (Packages, Redesign, later Tradie Websites) · **Hosting & Care** · **SEO** · **AI Marketing** · **About** · **Get a Quote**. Put guides and eventual portfolio in secondary navigation. Use a compact homepage with about five main content sections.

Do not create separate generic Web Design and Small Business Web Design pages at launch. The homepage owns that intent. Do not launch a suburb/city page grid. New pages must answer a distinct need with genuine substance.

### Decision rule after quantitative validation

Promote a cluster when it has clear buying intent, relevant measurable or directly observed demand, and a realistic page-quality opportunity. Retain core service pages even when they are unlikely to rank quickly, because visitors still need to understand the offer. Defer speculative verticals when their demand is uncertain and producing credible examples would be costly. Use enquiries and sales, not raw traffic, to decide what to expand.

### Guide 1 — Understanding website costs

**URL:** `/guides/website-costs/`  
**Title:** Small Business Website Costs: What to Compare  
**Meta description:** Compare the build, hosting, maintenance and software costs in a small business website quote. Understand what is included before choosing a package.

# What should a small business website quote include?

Two website quotes can look similar while covering very different work. One might include content writing and launch support. Another may cover the design alone, leaving you to organise the words, images and ongoing services.

The useful question is what your business receives for the price—and what you will pay after launch.

## Separate the build from the ongoing costs

The build covers the work needed to create or redesign the website. This may include planning, page design, content, development and testing. Ask for the page count, features, review process and responsibilities to be written down.

Ongoing costs may include domain renewal, hosting, business email, maintenance and paid software. Some providers bundle these; others invoice separately. Compare the total over the same period, rather than comparing an upfront quote with a monthly subscription.

## Check who prepares the content

Someone needs to explain your services, organise project examples and check the business facts. Find out whether your quote includes writing, editing or simply inserting the text you supply. Ask whether images are included and whether you can use them after the project ends.

## Understand the meaning of “SEO included”

It might mean basic page titles and setup. It might include keyword research and service copy. It does not necessarily mean ongoing content, technical reviews or monthly reporting. Ask for the activities to be listed rather than relying on the label.

## Ask about access and changes

Clarify who controls the domain, what website access you receive and what happens if you later move providers. Third-party software or licensed design elements may have their own conditions. Also ask which edits are included after launch and how larger changes are priced.

## Compare the same scope

Before deciding, check these items side by side: pages; copy; images; forms; integrations; launch work; hosting; maintenance; subscriptions; access; and support. A quote becomes easier to judge when those details are visible.

### Guide 2 — Diagnosing a quiet website

**URL:** `/guides/website-not-getting-enquiries/`  
**Title:** Website Not Getting Enquiries? What to Check First  
**Meta description:** Work out whether your website needs more relevant visitors, clearer content or a better enquiry process before spending on a redesign or SEO.

# Why is your website not getting enquiries?

A quiet inbox can have several causes. People may not be finding the website. The visitors it receives may be looking for something else. Or interested customers may have trouble understanding your offer or contacting you.

Start by separating those problems.

## Check that enquiries can arrive

Submit a test enquiry from your phone and computer. Check that it reaches the correct inbox and that the visitor sees a genuine confirmation. Test your telephone and email links as well. A broken form can make a working marketing channel appear ineffective.

## Check what is actually being measured

No recorded traffic is not necessarily no visitors. Analytics may be missing or configured incorrectly. Search Console can help show Google search impressions and clicks, while website analytics describes tracked visits. Neither should be treated as a complete count of every possible interaction.

## If few relevant people reach the site

Check whether important pages can be accessed and indexed. Then review whether they describe the services and customer needs you want to be found for. A page that only says your business provides “solutions” may leave its actual offer unclear.

Search visibility takes more than changing the design. It can require stronger content, technical fixes, relevant promotion and time to evaluate the results.

## If visitors arrive but do not enquire

Read the page as someone choosing a provider. Can you identify the service, coverage area and next step quickly? Does the page answer practical questions? Are the work examples relevant and believable? Is the form asking for information that is unnecessary at this stage?

Check mobile use carefully. Small text, obstructive pop-ups and unclear contact links can make a simple enquiry unnecessarily difficult.

## Improve the part that is failing

If the site has little relevant visibility, investigate search and acquisition. If visitors are interested but cannot understand the offer, improve the content. If they struggle to submit the enquiry, fix the process. A redesign may help, but the evidence should determine the scope.
